11The sticker system — foundation piece 1 of 3

Foundation first, so everything downstream inherits it. Today the codebase has one sticker: a lemon pill with an ink border and a 2px offset. This turns it into a system — eight silhouettes, seven hues, three sizes, one keyline rule — so a sticker can be a category filter, a state flag, a milestone, a section marker or a decorative die-cut without ever being redrawn.

two keyline modes: die-cut (3px ground-coloured ring, for stickers lying loose on a surface) and stamped (2px ink border + 2px offset, for stickers that are controls) · ink text on every hue, no exceptions · rotation is chrome, never applied to a name or a number

the ring is painted two different ways — this is not optional

Five silhouettes are box-model shapes (circle, squircle, pill, oval, bubble) and take the ring as box-shadow: 0 0 0 3px {ground}. Three are clip-path shapes (starburst, rosette, diamond) and cannot — clip-path clips box-shadow, border and outline alike, so all three properties are cut away and the ring silently does not render. Those three get it from a nested pair sharing one clip-path: an outer element filled with the ground colour at full size and padding: 3px, holding an inner element with the same clip-path filled with the hue. The inner shape scales to its own inset box, so the ring is uniform. Do not try to fix a missing ring on a clipped sticker with a border — it will never appear.

The scale is not three chosen numbers, it is one number and two subtractions. Card padding is 10px, so 32 − 10 = 22 is the radius of anything sitting directly inside a card. Inner padding is 8px, so 22 − 8 = 14 is the radius of anything inside that. Nothing needs a fourth value, and the corner of a photo is always visibly parallel to the corner of the card holding it.
